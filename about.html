Personal Story

About Me

I'm Farah, a medical student at Damascus University, though most days I feel more like a researcher who happens to also be finishing medical school.

I got into research almost by accident. A single case report during my clinical years turned into a genuine curiosity: not just about diagnosing illness, but about why some people never get the chance to know what's wrong with them in the first place, or what to do about it. That curiosity turned into surveys with over a thousand participants, late nights with SPSS, and a growing list of questions I still don't have full answers to.

Outside of research, I spend a lot of my time in rooms that don't look like clinics; community centers, cultural halls, first-aid training spaces, trying to make health information make sense to people who were never given it in a form that fit them. Sometimes that means designing a curriculum for people with visual or hearing impairments. Sometimes it means training five medical students on how to ask a good research question. Sometimes it's just sitting with a group of parents and teachers, talking about how a community holds itself together after everything that's happened here.

I'm not someone who separates these things into "research" and "community". They feed each other. The questions I ask in a study usually started as something I noticed in a room full of people first.

I'm based in Rural Damascus, Syria, and I'm always happy to connect with people working on health literacy, disability-inclusive education, or anything in between.
